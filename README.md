# Beating the Odds: Countries That Live Longer Than Their Income Predicts

## Project Overview

This project explores why some countries achieve higher life expectancy than their income levels would suggest. We investigate the relationship between GDP per capita, life expectancy, healthcare expenditure, and other socioeconomic factors to understand why some countries perform better than expected.

## Research Question

Why do some countries achieve higher life expectancy than countries with similar income levels, and what factors might explain these differences?

## Target Audience

Our target audience is the general public, particularly people interested in global health, economic development, and inequality, with basic to intermediate data literacy.

### Primary Persona: Sarah

Sarah is a 32-year-old secondary school teacher from Nairobi, Kenya. She is interested in global development and understanding why people in some countries live longer than others. She wants to learn about global health inequalities through simple visualizations and relatable country comparisons.

**Presentation setting:** A public educational talk about global health and inequality.

## Five Potential Insights

1. Wealth matters, but it does not tell the whole story.
2. Countries with similar incomes can have different life expectancies.
3. Higher healthcare spending does not always guarantee longer lives.
4. Factors beyond income and healthcare expenditure may help explain better health outcomes.
5. Better health outcomes may be achievable without waiting for greater wealth.

## Main Insight

Some countries live approximately 5–7 years longer than their income levels would predict. Differences in healthcare spending may explain only part of this pattern, suggesting that other socioeconomic factors deserve further investigation.

## One-Sentence Summary

This project explores why some countries achieve higher life expectancy than their income levels would suggest, investigating how healthcare spending and other socioeconomic factors may contribute to their better-than-expected health outcomes.

## Narrative Summary

We begin by examining the relationship between national income and life expectancy across countries. Although wealthier countries generally have higher life expectancy, some countries achieve better outcomes than expected based on their economic resources.

We then highlight these countries and compare their healthcare spending to explore whether spending alone explains their performance. Finally, we investigate other possible contributing factors, such as education, healthcare access, sanitation, and living conditions.

Our goal is to show that national wealth is not the only factor associated with how long people live and to encourage a broader understanding of global health inequalities.
